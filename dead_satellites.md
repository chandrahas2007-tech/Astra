# Why dead satellites matter
Newton's cannonball: an orbit is a projectile that keeps missing the Earth. Two projectiles on crossing paths do not miss each other.
## Energy at orbital speed
- Crossing paths can close at more than 10 km/s
- Kinetic energy per kilogram: `½v² = ½ × (10,000 m/s)² = 50 MJ/kg`
- TNT releases about 4.2 MJ/kg, so each kilogram carries roughly 12 times that
## Who can dodge
- An active satellite can manoeuvre. An inactive one cannot
- Iridium 33 and Cosmos 2251 (2009): a working satellite hit a defunct one
## How dead satellites leave
- Drag lowers low orbits over time. Altitude and solar activity control how fast
- High orbits can last centuries, so operators move satellites to disposal orbits
## Limitations
- Inactive does not always mean uncontrolled. Check the finding notes before quoting numbers
